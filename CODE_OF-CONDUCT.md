# Code of Conduct

> **Every experiment deserves respect. Every contributor deserves dignity.**

## Our Purpose

RubberDuckWorks exists as an engineering laboratory where ideas are explored, challenged, documented, and improved.

We believe that good software is built through curiosity, collaboration, and thoughtful discussion rather than ego or hierarchy.

Every interaction should make the laboratory a better place for the next person who enters it.

---

# Our Principles

## 1. Be Respectful

Treat every contributor with professionalism and courtesy.

Disagreement is expected and often valuable, but personal attacks, harassment, intimidation, or discriminatory behavior have no place in this laboratory.

Critique ideas.

Never attack people.

---

## 2. Assume Good Intent

Most mistakes are the result of learning, experimentation, or incomplete information.

Ask questions before making assumptions.

Help before judging.

Teach before criticizing.

---

## 3. Document Your Thinking

Engineering is not only about producing code.

Explain decisions.

Document trade-offs.

Leave a trail that future contributors can understand.

A well-written explanation is often more valuable than a clever implementation.

---

## 4. Value Learning

This repository welcomes:

* beginners asking questions,
* experienced engineers sharing knowledge,
* experiments that fail,
* ideas that evolve over time.

Failure is acceptable.

Refusing to learn is not.

---

## 5. Prefer Clarity Over Cleverness

Readable code.

Readable documentation.

Readable conversations.

Future maintainability is more important than short-term brilliance.

---

## 6. Respect Time

Before asking for help:

* search existing documentation,
* read previous discussions,
* attempt a solution.

When helping others:

* provide context,
* explain reasoning,
* leave useful references.

---

# Expected Behaviour

Contributors are expected to:

* communicate professionally,
* welcome constructive feedback,
* acknowledge mistakes,
* cite sources when appropriate,
* improve documentation alongside implementation,
* support an inclusive learning environment.

---

# Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviours are incompatible with the values of RubberDuckWorks:

* harassment or discrimination,
* personal insults,
* trolling or inflammatory comments,
* deliberately misleading information,
* plagiarism or misrepresentation of another person's work,
* publishing confidential information without permission,
* hostile gatekeeping or dismissive behaviour toward learners.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

We believe that software engineering is a craft.

Craftsmanship requires:

* patience,
* discipline,
* curiosity,
* continuous improvement.

Every commit should leave the repository slightly better than it was found.

---

# Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to:

* repository discussions,
* issues,
* pull requests,
* documentation,
* project planning,
* code reviews,
* all collaborative spaces associated with RubberDuckWorks.

---

# Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for preserving a respectful and productive environment.

Actions may include:

1. private guidance,
2. formal warning,
3. temporary restriction,
4. removal from participation for repeated or severe violations.

Enforcement decisions should be consistent, documented, and focused on protecting the community rather than punishing individuals.

---

# Final Thought

RubberDuckWorks is more than a collection of files.

It is a laboratory for thinking.

We don't measure success by the amount of code written, but by the quality of ideas shared, the clarity of reasoning documented, and the willingness to help the next engineer understand a little more than they did yesterday.
