# ⚔️ Sprint 0D – Engineering Standards

## Mission

Establish a unified set of engineering standards that promote consistency, maintainability, quality, and long-term sustainability across every repository, project, experiment, and engineering activity within RubberDuckWorks.

---

## Why Engineering Standards Exist

Engineering standards transform governance into a daily practice.

While governance defines how engineering decisions are made, standards define how those decisions are consistently implemented.

Without common standards, repositories gradually diverge, documentation becomes inconsistent, architectural quality declines, and knowledge becomes increasingly difficult to maintain.

Engineering standards therefore exist to reduce unnecessary variation while preserving creativity where it matters most—problem-solving, architecture, and innovation.

Consistency should never restrict engineering thought.

It should eliminate avoidable friction.

---

## Engineering Standards

### I. Repository Consistency

Every repository shall follow the established RubberDuckWorks structure whenever practical.

Directory organization, naming conventions, documentation layout, and engineering artifacts should remain predictable across projects.

Consistency improves discoverability, onboarding, and long-term maintainability.

---

### II. Documentation First

Engineering documentation is considered part of the implementation—not an afterthought.

Every meaningful architectural decision, engineering process, and significant implementation should be accompanied by documentation appropriate to its scope.

Code explains how.

Documentation explains why.

---

### III. Quality Before Quantity

Engineering quality takes precedence over implementation speed.

A smaller, well-understood solution is preferred over a larger solution that introduces unnecessary complexity.

Technical debt should be consciously accepted—not accidentally created.

---

### IV. Traceable Engineering

Significant engineering work should be traceable from its original motivation through implementation and review.

Issues, milestones, ADRs, pull requests, and documentation together create an engineering history that future contributors can understand.

---

### V. Continuous Improvement

Engineering standards are expected to evolve.

As RubberDuckWorks matures, standards should be reviewed, refined, and expanded based upon practical experience rather than theoretical perfection.

Standards themselves are living engineering artifacts.

---

## Engineering Expectations

RubberDuckWorks encourages engineers to:

- Understand before implementing.
- Document before forgetting.
- Improve before expanding.
- Review before merging.
- Learn before repeating.

Engineering excellence is achieved through disciplined iteration rather than isolated moments of brilliance.

---

## Outcome

RubberDuckWorks now possesses a documented engineering standard that establishes consistent expectations for documentation, repository organization, engineering quality, and continuous improvement across every future project.
