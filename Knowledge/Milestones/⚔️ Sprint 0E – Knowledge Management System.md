# ⚔️ Sprint 0E – Knowledge Management System

## Mission

Establish a structured knowledge management system that preserves engineering experience, architectural reasoning, research, experimentation, and lessons learned as permanent assets within RubberDuckWorks.

---

## Why Knowledge Management Exists

Software evolves.

Technologies change.

Architectures mature.

Engineering knowledge should evolve with them.

Without deliberate knowledge management, valuable experience gradually disappears as projects change direction, contributors move on, and implementation details fade from memory.

RubberDuckWorks treats knowledge as a long-term engineering asset that deserves the same level of structure and care as source code.

Knowledge accumulated today should speed up engineering tomorrow.

---

## Knowledge Principles

### I. Knowledge is Permanent

Engineering knowledge should survive individual projects, technologies, and implementation details.

Lessons learned remain valuable even after software has changed.

---

### II. Experience Becomes Documentation

Research findings, experiments, architectural discoveries, failures, reviews, and successful practices should be captured as structured knowledge.

Experience gains value only when it becomes reusable.

---

### III. Knowledge Must Be Discoverable

Information should be organized so future contributors can locate it without relying on personal memory.

Well-structured knowledge reduces repeated investigation and duplicated effort.

---

### IV. Knowledge Evolves

Documentation is never considered complete.

As understanding improves, knowledge should be refined, corrected, expanded, and reorganized.

A living knowledge base reflects a living engineering system.

---

### V. Knowledge Enables Engineering

The purpose of knowledge management is not documentation for its own sake.

Its purpose is to enable better engineering decisions, reduce uncertainty, and speed up continuous learning.

Knowledge exists to improve future engineering.

---

## Knowledge Domains

RubberDuckWorks organizes knowledge into complementary domains, including:

- Engineering methodology
- Architecture
- Governance
- Standards
- Research
- Experiments
- Reviews
- Project history
- Milestones
- Lessons learned

Together these domains create a continuously expanding engineering knowledge system.

---

## Outcome

RubberDuckWorks now possesses a structured knowledge management framework capable of preserving engineering experience, supporting continuous learning, and enabling long-term architectural evolution across the entire engineering laboratory.
